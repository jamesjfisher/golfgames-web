Wolf

On every hole one player is the Wolf. After watching the others tee off, the Wolf picks a partner for the hole, or goes it alone against everyone for more points. The role moves on every hole, so everybody gets their turn.

Players4 or 5. With 5, the Wolf and partner play 2 against 3.
ScoringBest ball on each side decides the hole. Points per the table you choose.
MoneyWhat's a point worth: Free, 25¢, $1, $2, $5 or Other. $1 is the default.

Setting it up

On the Format step, add Wolf ("A rotating Wolf picks a partner, or goes alone") and open its options.

  • Who's playing: 4 or 5 of the group. Anyone left out keeps their own card and sits this game out.
  • Tee order: the order you drive in, which is also the Wolf rotation. Hole 1's Wolf is first, hole 2's is second, and so on, wrapping around. Move players up or down, then tap Use this order. The round can't start until you do.
  • Closing holes: Keep rotating (the default) or Last place is Wolf. See Closing holes below.
  • Scoring (Gross or Net) and Handicap allowance. In a net game each player gets strokes off their own handicap, after the allowance, then each side takes its best ball.
  • Points: Classic (the default), Simple, or Custom to set each value yourself. See the point tables.
  • What's a point worth: the amount each point is paid at.
  • Settlement (shown once a point is worth more than $0): Each pair (the default) or Split the pot. See Settling up.
  • Who can press Coming in the next app update (shown once a point is worth more than $0): Whoever's down (the default), the game's own rule, or Anyone, "Anyone in the game can press once a hole has been scored, even when they are ahead. Nobody has to agree to it." The game's summary line then ends "anyone can press". See Presses.
  • Carry ties over (off by default): "When on, a tied hole doubles the next one instead of washing."
  • Low total point (off by default): a second bet on every partnered hole. See Low total point.
  • Blind Wolf allowed (on by default): lets the Wolf go solo before anyone tees off, for the most points.
  • Partner can turn down the Wolf Coming in the next app update (off by default): "If they do, they play the hole alone as the Lone Wolf against everyone else". See Turning the Wolf down.
  • Take the call in tee order (off by default): "Scoring asks about one player at a time, in the order they drive, instead of showing every partner at once. A player passed over is out for that hole."

Wolf can't share a round with Four-Ball, Abilene, Banker, 6-6-6 or Vegas.

How a hole is scored

The Wolf tees off first and then decides. On the hole screen, the Wolf band asks "Who did they take?". Tap one player's name, or one of:

  • A partner: the Wolf and that player play their best ball against the best ball of the rest, "the pack".
  • Lone Wolf: the Wolf plays alone against everyone, after seeing the tee shots.
  • Blind: the Wolf goes alone before anyone tees off. It can only be chosen while the hole has no scores yet.

Each side counts its lowest score. The lower side wins the hole and its players take the points. A tied hole scores nothing for anyone, unless Carry ties over is on.

  • No call, no points: a hole where nobody recorded the Wolf's choice scores nothing, even with every score in. The band says so: "No call yet — this hole scores nothing for anyone until somebody is named."
  • Changing the call: tap a different choice ("Change the call:") at any time to correct a mistake. Blind still needs a hole with no scores.
  • Carry ties over: each tie adds one to the next hole's multiplier, so one tie doubles the next hole and two in a row triple it. A hole nobody called doesn't carry.

Turning the Wolf down Coming in the next app update

With Partner can turn down the Wolf on, the player the Wolf picks gets a say. Once a partner is tapped, the band shows a second row, "Sam's answer", with In with Alex already selected and Turn down · Lone 4 beside it (the number is your Lone Wolf points).

  • Turning it down: the picked player plays the hole alone as the Lone Wolf against everyone else, including the Wolf who picked them. The band reads "Sam turned down Alex".
  • It pays exactly as a Lone Wolf does: Lone Wolf wins if they win, Beat a solo Wolf to each of the others if they lose. There is no separate price.
  • Only before the hole has a score. Once anyone's score is down on the hole, the answer row goes away. Undo puts the partner back in with the Wolf, under the same rule: "A turn-down can only be undone before anyone has a score on this hole."
  • Lone and Blind calls can't be turned down; there is no partner to answer.
  • With Take the call in tee order on, the prompt asks "Alex took Sam. Is Sam in?" with Sam's in and Sam turns it down · Lone 4.
  • The hole still counts as the original Wolf's turn. On the scorecard's Wolf row, the player who turned it down is shown with a ✕ after their name.

"Each" means every player on the winning side gets that many. Beat a solo Wolf is what the pack scores for beating a Lone Wolf, and Beat a blind Wolf for beating a Blind Wolf. The blind row shows only while Blind Wolf allowed is on. On Custom it starts at double Beat a solo Wolf and follows it until you change it. Only the winning side is given points. The settle-up then turns everyone's points into money against each other.

Low total point

With Low total point on, every partnered hole carries a second bet. Each side adds its best 2 scores, and the lower total wins the same points again from the table. With five players the pack counts its best 2 of 3.

  • Lone and Blind Wolf holes are low ball only.
  • A tied total pays nothing and never carries. Only a tied low ball carries.
  • The two bets can go to opposite sides on the same hole. The scorecard's Wolf tab then shows a Low ball row and a Total row.

Closing holes

Eighteen holes don't divide evenly by four or five, and nor do nine, so some players get an extra turn as Wolf.

  • Keep rotating: the order carries on to the last hole. With four players, the first two in the order are Wolf again on 17 and 18.
  • Last place is Wolf: everybody gets the same number of turns, then the leftover holes go to whoever is down the most money when the hole starts. With four players that's 17 and 18, and with five it's 16, 17 and 18. If two are tied for last, the group picks.
  • Nine holes: with four players the leftover is the last hole, and with five it's the last four (6, 7, 8 and 9 on a front nine; 15, 16, 17 and 18 on a back nine).

Every new round starts on Keep rotating; the app doesn't remember the last choice. So the game's summary line on the Format step says which rule you have and which holes it covers, for example "Wolf keeps rotating on 17 and 18" or "last place is Wolf on 6, 7, 8 and 9". Check it before you tee off. Coming in the next app update

Presses

A press raises what a point is worth for the whole game, from a hole you choose to the end of the round. The sheet is titled Press the bet.

  • Who can press: "Only the player in last place can press, and the stake can never come back down." Last place is counted in money, with earlier presses included. Ties for last can all press. With Who can press set to Anyone, the sheet says "Anyone can press, ahead or behind, and the stake can never come back down." Every player is offered the press once a hole has a score.
  • From which hole: Starts on can be any hole of the round, including one already played. Holes before it keep their old amount.
  • Amount: Dollars a point, at least one step above the amount in force: $1 a step, or 25¢ a step below $1.
  • Not offered on a Wolf played for nothing. Nobody can press until a hole has a score.

In the app

Partway through a round, the scorecard shows everyone's holes. Tap Wolf under YOUR GAMES to see each player's points and money, and Press the bet. These screens are from a different round than the worked example above.

Settling up

  • Each pair: "Every player settles with every other on the difference in points, so a point is worth the stake from each player behind you."
  • Split the pot: "Each hole is settled on its own. The winners collect their points at the stake, and the losers share the cost evenly." With Low total point on, each bet on a hole is its own pot. A share that doesn't divide evenly is rounded to whole cents, and the round still adds up to zero.
  • Presses: each hole is paid at the amount in force for it.
  • At the end of the round, only holes that were decided count. A hole nobody called, or one still missing a score, pays nothing.

Good to know

  • Picking up gives your side no ball. If your partner or the pack still has a score, that's the side's ball; a side with no ball at all loses the hole. In the low total, a pick-up counts as a net double bogey.
  • Five players: "Five in the Wolf plays 2 against 3 — the Wolf still takes one partner, and the other three play as the pack." A Lone Wolf plays all four.
  • The Wolf is fixed once the hole is called. Correcting an earlier score later doesn't change who was Wolf, even on a Last place is Wolf hole.
