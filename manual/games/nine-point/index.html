9-Point

A game for a threesome. Every hole is worth 9 points: the best score takes 5, the next 3 and the worst 1. It's sometimes called Nines or 5-3-1.

PlayersExactly 3
Scoring9 points a hole, split 5-3-1 by finishing order
MoneyWhat a point is worth: Free, $0.25, $1, $2 or Custom. $1 is the default.

Setting it up

On the Format step, add 9-Point and open its options.

  • Who's playing: exactly three. "9-Point is played by exactly three. Anyone else in the group keeps their own card and sits this bet out." With only two in the group it's listed as needing 3 players.
  • Scoring (Gross or Net) and Handicap allowance. Net uses the difference: the lowest handicap of the three plays off scratch and the others get the difference, after the allowance.
  • Sweep 9-0-0 (off by default): "Beat both others by 2 strokes or more and take all 9 points."
  • Birdie bonus 7-1-1 (off by default): "The only birdie or better on a hole scores 7-1-1. Counted gross, even in a net game."
  • What a point is worth: the amount each point is paid at.
  • Settlement (shown once a point is worth more than $0): Each pair (the default) or Split the pot. See Settling up.

How a hole is scored

Once all three have a score, the hole's 9 points are shared out:

  • Finishing order: 5 to the best score, 3 to the next, 1 to the worst.
  • Ties share the places they fill: two tied for best take 4 each and the third takes 1 (4-4-1). Two tied for worst take 2 each behind a 5 (5-2-2). All three tied take 3 each (3-3-3).
  • Sweep, if on: a player who beats both others by 2 strokes or more takes all 9 (9-0-0).
  • Birdie bonus, if on: when exactly one player makes birdie or better (gross), the hole scores 7-1-1, whatever the order of the other two. If two players make birdie, it's scored by finishing order.
  • Both on: a sweep beats a birdie.

The hole screen shows the split as soon as the third score goes in, for example 5-3-1, Tie · 4-4-1, Birdie bonus · 7-1-1 or Sweep · 9-0-0.

Presses

A press raises what a point is worth for the whole game, from a hole you choose to the end of the round. It works exactly like Wolf's. The sheet is titled Press the bet.

  • Who can press: only the player in last place, counted in money with earlier presses included. Players tied for last can all press.
  • From which hole: Starts on can be any hole of the round, including one already played. Holes before it keep their old amount, and the amount can never come back down.
  • Amount: Dollars a point, at least one step above the amount in force: $1 a step, or 25¢ a step below $1.
  • Not offered on a 9-Point played for nothing. Nobody can press until a hole has a score.

  • Hole 2: Alex and Sam tie for best and share 5 + 3, 4 each.

  • Hole 4: Sam's birdie 4 beats both others by 2 or more. That's a sweep, and a sweep beats a birdie: 9-0-0.

In the app

Partway through a round, the scorecard shows everyone's holes. Tap 9-Point under YOUR GAMES to see each player's points and money, and Press the bet. These screens are from a different round than the worked example above.

Settling up

  • Each pair: "Each pair settles the difference in their points, so 5-3-1 pays +6, 0 and −6."
  • Split the pot: "Each hole is settled against its average of 3 points, so 5-3-1 pays +2, 0 and −2." It always comes to a third of the Each pair figure.
  • Presses: each hole is paid at the amount in force for it.
  • At the end of the round, only holes all three finished count. A partial or nine-hole round settles what was played.

Good to know

  • Picking up scores a net double bogey for the hole. If all three pick up, it's 3-3-3.
  • The Birdie bonus is always gross. In a net game, a gross birdie counts for whoever makes it, strokes or not, and a net birdie doesn't.
  • An eagle counts as a birdie for the bonus.
