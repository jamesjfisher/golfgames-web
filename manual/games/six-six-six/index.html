6-6-6

A game for a foursome in pairs. The round is split into short matches called segments, and the partners change at every one. Over 18 holes you partner each of the other three once.

PlayersExactly 4, in two pairs that change every segment
Round length18 holes: three 6-hole segments (the default) or six 3-hole segments. 9 holes: 3-hole segments only.
ScoringEach segment is a match. Each pair's better ball (or both balls added) wins or halves each hole.
MoneyWhat a segment is worth: Free, $2, $5, $10 or Custom, in whole dollars. $5 is the default. Every player puts it up for every segment.

Setting it up

On the Format step, add 6-6-6 ("Partners rotate — three short matches, everyone with everyone") and open its options.

  • Who's playing: exactly four. "6-6-6 is played by exactly four — two pairs, swapped every segment. Anyone else keeps their own card and is out of this bet."
  • Rotation: the order of the four players, which decides who partners whom. Call them A, B, C and D. The first segment is A & B against C & D, the second A & C against B & D, and the third A & D against B & C. Move players with the arrows, then tap Use this rotation. The round can't start until you do.
  • How long is a segment: 6 holes (the default), "Three matches of six. Everyone partners everyone exactly once." Or 3 holes, "Six matches of three. The rotation runs twice, so every pairing plays twice." A 9-hole round needs 3 holes: "9 holes will not divide into 6-hole segments. Shorten the segments, or go back and play all 18."
  • How a hole is scored: Better ball (the default), "The pairing takes its better score on each hole." Or Both balls, "The pairing adds both scores on each hole."
  • Scoring (Gross or Net) and Handicap allowance. Net uses the difference: the lowest handicap of the four plays off scratch and the others get the difference, after the allowance.
  • What a segment is worth: the amount each player puts up for each segment. The sheet shows how often, for example "Each player puts this up every segment — 3 times over 18 holes."

6-6-6 can't share a round with Four-Ball, Wolf, Abilene, Banker or Vegas.

How a segment is scored

Each segment is its own match play between the two pairs.

  • On each hole, each pair takes its better score, or adds both scores with Both balls. The lower pair wins the hole. Equal scores halve it.
  • The pair ahead at the end of the segment wins it. A segment can be decided early, for example 2&1 in a 3-hole segment. A segment that finishes level is a push and pays nothing.
  • Then the partners change. The next segment starts level, with the next pairing from the rotation.

Presses

A press is a second bet inside one segment. The sheet is titled Press the segment.

  • Who can press: "A side at least one down can press. It is a separate bet for the same amount over the rest of that segment, and it ends when the segment does." Either player in the pair can call it.
  • From which hole: the next hole without a score. A 6-6-6 press can't start on a hole already played.
  • Amount: the segment's own amount. There's nothing to choose.
  • One press per segment. No press on a segment that's already decided, or on a 6-6-6 played for nothing.

In the app

Partway through a round, the scorecard shows everyone's holes. Tap 6-6-6 under YOUR GAMES to see each six-hole segment, its partners and result. These screens are from a different round than the worked example above.

Settling up

  • Each segment: each player in the winning pair is up the amount, and each player in the losing pair is down it. A halved segment pays nothing.
  • Each press pays the same way, at its own amount.
  • Your total is the sum of every segment and press you played.
  • At the end of the round, a segment or press that hasn't been decided pays nothing, even if one pair is ahead in it.

Good to know

  • Picking up with Better ball: your partner's score is the pair's ball. If both partners pick up, the pair has no ball and loses the hole, or halves it if the other pair has none either.
  • Picking up with Both balls: the pair has no total for the hole, so it loses the hole, or halves it if the other pair has no total either.
  • The net strokes come off the low player's handicap, not your own. The lowest handicap gets none, so you may see fewer strokes on your card than in a game that uses your own handicap.
