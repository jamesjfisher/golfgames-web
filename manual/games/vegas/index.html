Vegas

A team game for four in which both partners' scores count. A team's two scores are written as one number, low first, so a 4 and a 5 make 45. The lower number wins the hole, and the hole is worth the difference.

PlayersExactly 4, in two teams of two
ScoringEach team's two scores make one number. The lower number wins the difference in points.
MoneyWhat a point is worth: Free, $0.10, $0.25, $1 or Custom. $0.25 is the default.

Setting it up

On the Format step, add Vegas ("Both partners count — two scores make one number, low first") and open its options.

  • Who's playing: exactly four. Anyone else in the group keeps their own card and sits this bet out.
  • Teams: the order of the four players. The first two play the last two: "These two pairs play each other for the whole round." Move players with the arrows, then tap Use these teams. The round can't start until you do. When partners change during the round, this row is called Rotation, sets the first pairing, and the button reads Use this rotation.
  • How often partners change: Whole round (the default), "Partners are fixed. The two teams you pick below play each other all the way." Every 9, "Partners are re-drawn at the turn, so you play two of the three pairings." Or Every 6, "Partners are re-drawn every six holes, so everyone partners everyone." All three work on a 9-hole round.
  • Scoring (Gross or Net) and Handicap allowance. Net uses the difference: the lowest handicap of the four plays off scratch and the others get the difference, after the allowance.
  • What a point is worth: "Dollars a point, and a hole is worth the difference between the two team numbers." A hole is often worth 10 or 20 points, so a small amount goes a long way.

Vegas can't share a round with Four-Ball, Wolf, Abilene, 6-6-6 or Banker.

How a hole is scored

Once all four have a score, the hole is worked out in this order:

  1. Each team's number: its two scores, lower first. A 4 and a 5 is 45; a 3 and a 6 is 36. If either score is 10 or more, the higher one goes first: a 4 and a 12 is 124, not 412.
  2. The birdie flip: a natural birdie flips the other team's number, higher score first, so their 47 becomes 74. A natural birdie is a birdie or better made without a handicap stroke on the hole. If both teams have one, nothing flips. A number already written high first stays as it is.
  3. The lower number wins. The hole is worth the difference: 45 against 56 is worth 11 points. Equal numbers halve the hole for nothing.
  4. Two birdies double it. If both players on the winning team made a natural birdie, the hole's points double.

Each player on the winning team gains the points, and each player on the losing team loses them.

When partners change

With the players in order A, B, C, D, the pairings are A & B against C & D, then A & C against B & D, then A & D against B & C.

  • Whole round: A & B against C & D for every hole.
  • Every 9: the first pairing plays holes 1–9, the second holes 10–18.
  • Every 6: one pairing each for holes 1–6, 7–12 and 13–18. On a 9-hole round, the first pairing plays 1–6 and the second plays 7–9.

Vegas has no presses.

In the app

Partway through a round, the scorecard shows everyone's holes. Tap Vegas under YOUR GAMES to see each player's points and money. These screens are from a different round than the worked example above.

Settling up

  • Your points times the amount. Points are kept per player, so with rotating partners each player's total is their own.
  • At the end of the round, a hole without all four scores pays nothing. A partial or nine-hole round settles what was played.

Good to know

  • Picking up still gives your team a digit: a picked-up ball scores net double bogey, the par plus two plus any strokes you get on the hole. It never counts as a birdie.
  • In a net game, a birdie made with a handicap stroke on the hole doesn't flip anything, and no score counts below 1.
  • The net strokes come off the low player's handicap, not your own, as in 6-6-6 and 9-Point.
